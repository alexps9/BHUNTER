BHUNTER

Generating Blind-Spot Driving Scenarios for Robustness Testing of Autonomous Driving Perception

BHUNTER searches simulated traffic until a road user falls into a chosen blind spot, then exports those scenes for detection and tracking.

Motivation

  • Everyday driving logs contain few moments in which a road user slips behind another object and then reappears. Perception models are rarely scored on that gap.
  • A simulator can sample traffic at random, but random traffic does not aim at a chosen amount of occlusion.
  • A detector or tracker can look reliable while the target is fully visible and still lose it once the same target is heavily blocked.
  • BHUNTER searches for those missing views inside CARLA and keeps the scenarios whose occlusion matches the requested level.

Method

Search starts from a corpus of scenario seeds: a map, weather, an ego route, and a candidate actor. The fuzzer mutates the surrounding traffic, resimulates the scene, and scores how much of the candidate is occluded. Guided search keeps variants that move toward the requested occlusion. Random search and a weaker schedule are included as alternatives.

Scenes that reach the target are written out in a KITTI-style layout, with camera images, LiDAR, calibration, labels, and, for tracking, poses. A separate appearance-transfer model, adapted from Unsup Recycle-GAN, can shift simulated frames toward real imagery. The released checkpoint is epoch 50.

Benchmark

The repository ships the KITTI-style benchmark under evaluation/benchmark, split into detection and tracking. Each split has the five occlusion levels below.

No occlusion

The candidate stays in open view.

Low

A small part of the candidate is blocked.

Moderate

Occlusion covers a substantial share of the target.

Severe

Most of the candidate is hidden from the ego vehicle.

Extreme

The candidate is nearly out of sight.

Each level includes calibration, RGB images, labels, ground planes, and LiDAR. Tracking also includes poses and an occlusion window.
